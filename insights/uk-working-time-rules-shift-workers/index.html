UK working time rules for shift workers: a guide for managers

A plain-English guide to the Working Time Regulations for UK shift-based businesses: the 48-hour week, rest between shifts, breaks, night work, young workers and holiday.

By BTree · 24 November 2026 · 7 minute read

If you run a restaurant, shop, care home or warehouse, your rota has to respect the Working Time Regulations 1998. This guide sets out the main rules for workers in Great Britain in plain English. Northern Ireland has its own, very similar rules.

This is general information, not legal advice. Rules change, so check GOV.UK or Acas for your situation.

The 48-hour week

Workers aged 18 and over cannot be made to work more than 48 hours a week on average. The average is normally worked out over 17 weeks, so a busy week can be balanced by quieter ones.

  • A worker can choose to opt out of the 48-hour limit. The opt-out must be voluntary and in writing, and they can cancel it by giving notice.
  • You cannot dismiss or treat someone unfairly for refusing to opt out.
  • Workers under 18 cannot opt out.

Rest between shifts

Adult workers are entitled to 11 hours rest in a row between working days. A close-then-open pattern, such as finishing at 23:00 and starting again at 07:00, breaks this rule.

Weekly rest

Adult workers are also entitled to an uninterrupted 24 hours without work each week, or 48 hours each fortnight.

Breaks during a shift

Adults working more than 6 hours in a day are entitled to one uninterrupted 20-minute break during the shift, not at the start or end. It does not have to be paid unless the contract says so.

Young workers (under 18)

Workers above school leaving age but under 18 have stronger protection:

  • no more than 8 hours a day and 40 hours a week, with no averaging and no opt-out;
  • 12 hours rest between working days and 48 hours rest each week;
  • a 30-minute break if they work more than 4.5 hours;
  • generally no work between 10pm and 6am (or 11pm and 7am if the contract says so). In some sectors, such as hotels and catering, retail and bakeries, they can work some of those hours, but generally not between midnight and 4am.

Night workers

Someone who regularly works at least 3 hours during the night period (normally 11pm to 6am) is a night worker. Night workers should not work more than 8 hours in each 24 on average, and must be offered a free health assessment. They cannot opt out of the night work limit.

Holiday

Almost all workers are entitled to 5.6 weeks of paid holiday a year. For someone working five days a week that is 28 days, which can include bank holidays. For irregular-hours and part-year workers, in leave years starting on or after 1 April 2024, holiday builds up at 12.07% of the hours worked in each pay period.

Keeping records

You must keep records that show you are meeting the weekly limit and the night work limits, and keep them for two years. Copies of any opt-out agreements should be kept too.
