Cyber Essentials explained: a plain-English guide for UK small businesses

What Cyber Essentials and Cyber Essentials Plus are, the five technical controls, who needs certification and how a small business can prepare to pass first time.

By BTree · 24 November 2026 · 6 minute read

Cyber Essentials is a UK government-backed scheme that helps organisations protect themselves against the most common online attacks. It is run by the National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) and delivered by IASME through a network of certification bodies. For many small businesses it is the most sensible first step in cyber security, and more and more customers ask their suppliers for it.

Cyber Essentials and Cyber Essentials Plus

  • Cyber Essentials is a self-assessment. You answer an online questionnaire about your IT, and a certification body reviews your answers.
  • Cyber Essentials Plus covers the same controls, but an assessor also checks them in person or remotely, for example by testing a sample of your devices. It has to be completed within three months of the basic certification.

Certification lasts for 12 months, so you renew every year.

The five controls

  1. Firewalls. Every device and network connected to the internet is protected by a properly configured firewall.
  2. Secure configuration. Default passwords are changed, unnecessary software and accounts are removed, and devices lock automatically.
  3. Security update management. Software is supported by its maker and kept up to date. High-risk and critical updates must be installed within 14 days.
  4. User access control. People only have the access they need, admin accounts are used only for admin tasks, and multi-factor authentication is switched on for cloud services.
  5. Malware protection. Anti-malware software or application allow-listing protects every device in scope.

What is in scope

Usually your whole organisation: office computers, laptops, phones and tablets that access company data, servers, network equipment and cloud services such as Microsoft 365. Home workers' devices, and personal devices used for work, are normally in scope too. You can certify only part of the organisation, but it must be clearly separated.

Who needs it?

Cyber Essentials is required for some UK central government contracts, particularly where personal information or certain ICT services are involved. Many larger businesses also ask their suppliers for it, and it gives you a clear, independent baseline to show customers and insurers.

Common reasons businesses fail

  • Unsupported software, such as computers still running an operating system the maker no longer updates.
  • Multi-factor authentication missing on one or more cloud services.
  • Updates not installed within 14 days.
  • Staff using admin accounts for email and web browsing.
  • Default passwords left on routers, firewalls or other devices.

How to prepare

  1. List every device, account and cloud service the business uses.
  2. Replace or upgrade anything that is out of support.
  3. Turn on multi-factor authentication for every cloud service.
  4. Set devices to update automatically, and check that they do.
  5. Remove admin rights from everyday accounts.
  6. Read the current question set from IASME before you start the assessment.
